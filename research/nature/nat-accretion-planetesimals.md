> **Strict analogy.** Dust has no agents or intent; the process is physics.

## Mechanism
- During planet formation in circumstellar gas disks, dust grains collide and build larger bodies; **from metre-size boulders to km-scale planetesimals is "a major unsolved problem"** — boulders stick poorly and spiral inward in a few hundred orbits owing to gas headwind (abstract).
- Simulation result: boulders **concentrate in transient high-pressure regions of turbulent gas; a streaming instability driven by the relative flow of gas and solids augments this by a further order of magnitude**; gravitationally bound clusters form with masses comparable to dwarf planets; gravitational collapse is much faster than radial drift (abstract).

## Evidence
`author-reported` (numerical simulation; not an observation of a forming planet).

## Correction for the landing page (realms.mjs)
"Dust and gas clump into planets through countless local collisions": the metre-to-kilometre step is **not** explained by collisions alone — the source argues a collective gravitational collapse of locally overdense clumps is needed. Suggested: "Dust grains stick into boulders through collisions, then clump collectively — through a streaming instability and gravitational collapse — into planetesimals (a simulation result)."

## Engineering analogue
**Large structure from local aggregation, but not from local steps alone:** growth by pairwise merging stalls at a barrier; a collective instability crossing a density threshold gets past it. Look for such barriers when scaling a pool of agents by repeated pairwise merging.

## Limits of transfer
A simulation of fluid and solids, not a model of agents; do not infer a mechanism for software.
