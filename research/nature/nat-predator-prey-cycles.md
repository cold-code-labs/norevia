## Mechanism
- Yukon field experiment (8 years, 1 km² areas): predator exclosure doubled and food addition tripled hare density at peak and decline; both together raised density 11-fold; added nutrients raised plant growth but not hare density; the combined effect was more than additive, suggesting that **a three-trophic-level interaction generates hare cycles** (Krebs 1995, abstract).
- Fussmann 2000: in a two-species aquatic lab community, nonlinear interactions can **generate fluctuations internally**; the study crossed a predicted bifurcation between steady state and cycles in a live system (abstract, partial).

## Evidence
`natural-phenomenon` (field and laboratory experiments).

## Engineering analogue
**Elastic capacity by feedback, with risk of oscillation.** Spawning workers in response to backlog and retiring them in response to idleness is a feedback loop; with delays (spin-up time) it can overshoot and oscillate rather than settle. Measure the loop gain and delay before deploying elastic scaling.

## Testable hypothesis
- **Variable:** spawn rule — proportional to backlog with delay d vs smoothed with hysteresis.
- **Metric:** variance of active workers; cost; latency.
- **Prediction:** with large d the proportional rule oscillates; hysteresis damps it. Refuted if no oscillation appears.

## Limits of transfer
Population cycles have lagged births and deaths; agent pools can be resized almost instantly. The analogy applies mainly when provisioning delay is non-trivial.
