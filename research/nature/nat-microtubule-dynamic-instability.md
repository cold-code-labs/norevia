## Mechanism
- "Microtubules in vitro coexist in growing and shrinking populations which interconvert rather infrequently. This dynamic instability is a general property of microtubules and may be fundamental in explaining cellular microtubule organization" (Mitchison & Kirschner 1984, abstract, paraphrased).
- Correction to the landing-page wording: the source does not say microtubules "constantly" grow and collapse; it says individual polymers switch between growth and shrinkage **infrequently**, and that the population contains both.

## Evidence
`natural-phenomenon` (in vitro). Cellular function is the authors' proposal.

## Engineering analogue
**Provisional capacity:** keep structure (agents, caches, plans) that can be extended or dropped quickly because both states coexist in the pool; the system reorganises by changing which members grow or shrink, not by rebuilding. Complements `nat-clonal-expansion-contraction` (population scale) and `nat-inactive-reserve` (idle capacity).

## Limits of transfer
A molecular polymer property, shown here with a one-sentence abstract; do not extend to claims about rates or about cells without reading the full paper. Suggestive analogy only.
