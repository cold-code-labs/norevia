> **Strict analogy.** Stars do not "choose" to disperse.

## Mechanism
- The observed average lifetime of cluster populations (Solar Neighbourhood, SMC, regions of M 51 and M 33) is compared with simple theory and **N-body simulations**; the disruption time depends on **initial cluster mass** (and on the half-mass relaxation and crossing times) and on the **ambient density of the galaxy**, consistent with simulations (abstract).
- In M 51 within 1–5 kpc of the nucleus the disruption time is about **an order of magnitude shorter** than predicted — possibly because of strong tidal-field variations or disruptive forces from giant molecular clouds (abstract).

## Evidence
`author-reported` (observations compared with simulations/analytics; the explanation for M 51 is a possibility, not a result).

## Engineering analogue
**Every structure needs a Dissolve stage; its timescale depends on size and on the environment.** A group of agents has a lifetime governed by its size and by how crowded its environment is; planning the dissolution (not only creation) prevents leftover members. Complements `nat-clonal-expansion-contraction`.

## Limits of transfer
Dispersal here is gravitational and statistical. Not evidence for agent behaviour.
