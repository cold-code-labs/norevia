## Mechanism
- **Wolves/elk (Yellowstone, individually known wolves):** success was nonlinear in group size; **beyond 4 wolves hunting success levelled off**, and individual effort fell, consistent with **free-riding** (more among nonbreeders with no dependent offspring, those doing dangerous tasks, and those in groups of proficient hunters) (MacNulty 2011, abstract).
- **Dolphins (Cedar Key, FL):** in two groups, one individual was consistently the **"driver"** herding fish toward the remaining **"barrier"** dolphins; capture rates differed between roles in one group but not the other (Gazda 2005, abstract).
- **Counter-evidence (simulation):** two decentralised rules (approach prey to a safe distance; when close, move away from other close wolves) reproduce tracking, pursuit and encircling with **autonomous, interchangeable, indistinguishable agents and no hierarchy or communication** (Muro 2011, abstract). It shows what is sufficient in a model, not what wolves do.

## Evidence
`natural-phenomenon` for the field results; the simulation is `author-reported`. The "not a swarm" framing is Norevia's, not the sources'.

## Why this is a counter-model for H-04
Small groups with a plateau in success (wolves ≈ 4) and stable roles in some groups (dolphins) match the H-04 prediction that 2–3 agents suffice on decomposable tasks and larger groups add no acceptance. The free-riding result is the natural analogue of the "N > k adds cost, not acceptance" clause.

## Corrections for the landing page (realms.mjs, "Teams, not swarms")
- "few members, roles and individual recognition": **roles** are supported for the Cedar Key dolphins; for wolves the best-documented result is a success plateau with free-riding, and simulation shows roles are not needed; **individual recognition** is not covered by the abstracts read → drop it or add a source.
- Lions: unsourced here (gap).

## Limits of transfer
Observational field data (not randomised); two dolphin groups only; simulations show sufficiency, not mechanism. Do not claim that animal teams prove anything about LLM teams.
