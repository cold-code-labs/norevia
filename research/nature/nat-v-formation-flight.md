## Mechanism
- Imprinted great white pelicans trained to fly in V formation had lower heart rates (an estimate of energy expenditure): "these birds save a significant amount of energy by flying in formation" (Weimerskirch 2001, abstract).
- Bald ibis phase their wing flaps to exploit upwash and avoid downwash (Portugal 2014, abstract, partial).
- The leading bird cannot profit from upwash — a social dilemma. In juvenile Northern bald ibis on a human-guided migration, **time spent leading correlated strongly with time spent in another bird's wake**; pairs matched time by frequent switches of the leading position, suggesting cooperation by direct reciprocity (Voelkl 2015, abstract).

## Evidence
`natural-phenomenon` (trained and free-flying birds with instrumentation; author-reported).

## Engineering analogue
**Rotate the expensive role.** When one position in a pipeline (first reader of raw logs, the agent that takes uncertain first steps) carries the cost and others benefit from its output, rotating the role and tracking who owes turns avoids burnout of one agent or context; it also requires an observable record of who led.

## Testable hypothesis
- **Variable:** assignment of the costly front role — (A) fixed agent; (B) rotating by owed time.
- **Metric:** quality drift of the front role; total cost.
- **Prediction:** B limits drift in long runs; refuted if rotation overhead (hand-off of context) exceeds the benefit.

## Limits of transfer
Birds trade measured energy; agents trade tokens and context. Handing off a role costs context in agents but little in birds.
