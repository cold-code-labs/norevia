> **Evidence scope:** `local-validation` = validation **inside a simulated model** of abstract scouts. The model is inspired by quorum-based nest-site selection, but is not calibrated with biological data nor tested with LLMs.

## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-07T22:38:55Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `eececc9f1dd60ebe…`).

**Amendment history (all recorded in PREREGISTRATION.jsonl):**
- v1.0.0 → the 1st run showed **degenerate** dynamics: overly strong recruitment locked the colony onto the 1st site discovered (Q=50 in ~17–20 steps). Kept in the JSON as the `strong-recruitment` regime (same seeds).
- v1.1.0 (22:39:59Z, **post hoc**): new main regime `quality-weighted-recruitment`, parameters chosen on principle (only agents that perceive quality > 0.6 "dance"; recruitment 0.15; discovery 0.02; abandonment 0.02+0.2·(1−q̂)), without parameter search. Risk of fitting to the result: declared.
- v1.2.0 (22:40:19Z, **bug fix**): the tie-break in the quorum check favoured site 0 (the best) when several sites reached Q in the same step, inflating accuracy at small Q (Q=1: ~32% → 17–19% after the fix). Both regimes rerun.
- Hypothesis and criteria were never changed.

Requiring a larger quorum before deciding trades speed for accuracy with diminishing returns; when the agents' evaluations share a bias (correlated error), increasing the quorum does not correct the error and accuracy saturates below the independent case.

- **P1** — In every cell, accuracy non-decreasing in Q (tolerance 1 p.p.) and mean time to decision strictly increasing in Q.
- **P2** — Diminishing returns: where the gain from Q=1 to Q=50 is > 5 p.p., ≥ 50% of it already comes with Q ≤ 10.
- **P3** — With shared bias (ρ=0.5), accuracy at Q=50 lower than with ρ=0 (disjoint 95% CIs) and gain from Q=20→50 < 2 p.p. (plateau).
- **P4** — Δ=0.1 (hard discrimination) has lower accuracy than Δ=0.25 at every Q, for each (σ, ρ).

## Model
- 100 scouts; 3 sites with qualities [1; 1−Δ; 0.5]; the best is 3× harder to discover by chance.
- Uncommitted: is recruited to site j with prob. r·D_j/100 (D_j = sum of the "dance intensity" of the agents committed to j) or discovers a site on its own. Evaluates q̂ = q_j + σ(√ρ·B_j + √(1−ρ)·ε); commits if q̂ > 0.5. A committed agent abandons with prob. a₀ + a₁·max(0, 1−q̂).
- Main regime `quality-weighted-recruitment` (v1.1+): discovery 2%/step, r=0.15, dance = max(0, q̂−0.6)/0.4, a₀=0.02, a₁=0.2. Regime `strong-recruitment` (v1.0): discovery 1%/step, r=0.5, dance = q̂, a₀=0.01, a₁=0.1.
- Decision = first site to reach Q committed agents. B_j is a bias shared per site and run (everyone evaluates that site with the same deviation).
- 2000 runs per cell (σ ∈ {0.1; 0.3}, ρ ∈ {0; 0.5}, Δ ∈ {0.1; 0.25}); Q ∈ {1,…,50}. The quorum is "observed" (it does not alter the dynamics), so all Q values use the same run.

## Interpretation
- **The speed–accuracy trade-off exists at large scale** (Q=1→50: from ~18% to as much as 96–100% correct, from 1 to 45–78 steps), but **none of the four detailed predictions was supported**:
  - P1/P4 fail at Q ≤ 3: tiny quorums are decided by the **discovery lottery** (the middle site, 3× easier to find, is the first to reach 2–3 committed agents). Accuracy drops from Q=1 to Q=2–3 before rising.
  - P2 fails in the direction opposite to the prediction: the gain is **convex** (most of it comes from Q ≥ 20), not concave. Only after the colony has sampled several sites does quality-dependent recruitment single out the best.
  - P3 fails on the plateau criterion: with Q ≤ 50 the curve is still rising. The shared bias reduces accuracy where discrimination is easy (σ=0.3, Δ=0.25: 95.9% → 66.4%), but with small Δ and high σ it came out slightly higher (42.4% → 47.8%), still below the approximate ceiling of 63%.
- The `strong-recruitment` regime shows the failure mode most relevant to LLM swarms: **overly strong positive feedback = herding** — it decides fast (≈ 20 steps) and is wrong (≤ 50% even with Q=50).
- The conclusion depends strongly on the recruitment parameters; we treat the result as **inconclusive** for the general hypothesis.

## Limitations
- "Observed" quorum (it does not change behaviour, unlike bees, which switch mode on reaching quorum). No explicit distance/travel time. Parameters not calibrated with biological data.
- The main regime was defined after seeing the 1st run (post hoc).
- **What needs validation with real LLMs:** whether "stop when Q independent agents agree" has a convex or concave curve on real tasks; whether LLM agents that read each other's answers enter a herding regime (strong recruitment); effect of shared bias (same base model) on the quorum.
