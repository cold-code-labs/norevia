## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-07T22:37:09Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `db0528af72083d01…`).

A shared trail ("I have already explored here") reduces duplicated work and time to solution without messages; since agents do not know when an episode ends, old trail becomes noise, and evaporation works as forgetting: there is an optimal intermediate rate, and a trail without evaporation grows without bound.

- **P1** — For K=4 and K=16, stigmergy with ε=0.01 has lower time to solution and lower fraction of duplicated visits than no-coordination (disjoint 95% CIs).
- **P2** — U-shaped time in ε: there is 0<ε<1 with significantly lower time than ε=0 and ε=1, for K=4 and K=16.
- **P3** — All-to-all messages (perfect information, reset per episode) have the lowest time, at the cost of K(K−1) messages/step; the best ε is within ≤ 15% of the time of messages, with zero messages.
- **P4** — With ε=0 the trail mass grows ~linearly (unbounded memory) and the 10th episode is slower than the 1st; with the best ε the mass stabilises (mass ep.10/ep.5 < 1.2).

## Model
- 24×24 grid (576 nodes, 4-neighbourhood). K agents start at the centre. 10 episodes per run: a target appears at a uniform node; the episode ends when an agent steps on it; new target; agents continue from where they are.
- `no-coordination`: random walk without immediate return. `stigmergy`: moves to the neighbour with the least trail (trail decays by (1−ε) per step; +1 on arrival); agents do **not** know that the episode has ended. `all-to-all-messages`: same rule, but with a perfect visit count reset every episode, paid for with K(K−1) messages/step + end notice.
- By construction, `all-to-all-messages` is equivalent to an ideal trail erased at the start of each episode — the comparison isolates the value of **forgetting at the right time** and the price in messages.
- 500 runs × 10 episodes per configuration; the statistical unit is the run (mean over episodes).

## Interpretation
- **P1, P2, P4 supported.** The trail reduces time by ~2.5× and duplication from ~50% to ~14% vs no coordination. The curve in ε is U-shaped: without evaporation, the trail mass grows linearly (≈ 480/episode), old trail becomes noise and episodes get slower (K=4: 102 → 132 steps from the 1st to the 10th); with ε ≥ 0.01 (K=4) or ε ≥ 0.03 (K=16) the mass stabilises within a few episodes and time becomes stationary. With K=4 the difference between the best trail and messages is not significant (overlapping CIs); with K=16 the trail is significantly faster.
- **P3 not supported (on its 1st clause):** messages were not the minimum. The best trail (ε=0.1) was **equal or better** than the perfect per-episode count (ratio 0.98 with K=4 and 0.94 with K=16). Likely interpretation: with ε=0.1 the trail encodes *recency* ("someone passed here recently"), a better dispersion rule than the episode's accumulated count. Hence the comparison mixes information **and** decision rule; "perfect information" is not automatically the upper bound.
- ε=1 (trail vanishes within the same step) is worse than the uncoordinated walk because it loses even the "do not go back" memory — forgetting too fast also has a cost.
- Communication cost: all-to-all messages grow with K² (1,165 msgs/episode with K=4; 5,778 with K=16) for performance equivalent to the trail.

## Limitations
- Static, simple environment (grid without obstacles, single uniform target); the "least trail" rule is one of many possible; there is no cost for writing/reading the trail nor access contention.
- Repulsive trail (coverage); classic foraging with attractive pheromone and depleting sources was not modelled.
- **What needs validation with real LLMs:** whether a shared "I have already tried X" board with expiry reduces rework between LLM agents; token cost of reading the board vs direct messages; which "evaporation" (TTL) works for the typical horizon of a task.
