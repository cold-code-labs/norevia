## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-07T22:43:39Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `792e3930abd1a809…`).

An elastic rule that adds agents while the expected marginal gain (ΔP·V) exceeds the marginal cost, with ΔP estimated by a Bayesian model of difficulty, delivers lower cost per accepted task than any fixed N while maintaining the acceptance rate; the optimal size moves from 1 → micro → macro with task difficulty and value, and the rule only works well if the ΔP model accounts for error correlation.

- **P1** — For V ∈ {10, 20}, `elastic-seq` has lower cost per accepted task than every fixed-N (N=1,3,5,9; disjoint 95% CIs) and acceptance ≥ fixed-3 − 1 p.p.
- **P2** — For every V ∈ {2,5,10,20,50,100}, the net utility of `elastic-seq` is not significantly lower than that of the best fixed-N.
- **P3** — Optimal fixed N by class: easy → 1 (V ≥ 5); medium → micro 2..5 (V ∈ [10,50]); coupled → 0 (V ≤ 5) and macro ≥ 6 (V ≥ 50).
- **P4** — A misspecified controller (assumes ρ=0) has higher cost per accepted task than the calibrated one for V ≥ 20.
- **P5** — A difficulty probe (cost 0.05, 80% correct) increases utility vs `elastic-seq` for V ≥ 10.
- **P6** — `elastic-doubling` (batches 1,2,4,8,16) uses fewer rounds than `elastic-seq`, with cost per accepted task ≤ 1.2×, for V ≥ 10.

## Model
- Task mixture: easy 50% (p=0.9, ρ=0.2), medium 35% (p=0.5, ρ=0.3), coupled 15% (p=0.15, ρ=0.5). "Coupled" = low per-attempt success chance and highly correlated attempts; decomposition/specialisation **not** modelled.
- Each attempt costs 1 + 0.1 (acceptance test). Test: passes every correct answer, lets 2% of wrong ones through (false accept).
- V = value of an accepted, correct task, in units of attempt cost. Utility = V·[correct accepted] − cost − V·[false accept].
- `elastic-seq`: before each attempt, P(next correct | k failures) is computed by integration over (class, latent factor) — the posterior "learns" that the task is hard with each failure. Continues if P·V ≥ 1.1. Equivalent to a value-dependent attempt limit K*(V).
- 40,000 tasks per V, common random numbers across policies. Optimal fixed N per class computed exactly (integration).

- The **marginal value of the 2nd agent is maximal at p≈0.5** in every cell and decreases above that — a shape similar to the ~45% report, but here for a trivial reason: the gain of a 2nd attempt with an acceptance test is ∝ P(1st fails and 2nd succeeds) ≈ p(1−p), maximal at 0.5.
- Even so, with a reliable acceptance test, **N ≥ 2 remains optimal up to p = 0.6–0.95** (depending on V and ρ). That is, in the model, verified retrying does not "saturate" at 45%; the reported saturation requires mechanisms absent here (coordination overhead, error propagation, absence of a verifier).
- High ρ reduces the optimal N and the value of the 2nd agent (ρ=0.7, V=10: the swarm only pays off for p between 0.4 and 0.6).

## Interpretation
- **P2 supported with margin** — the main result: when maximising utility, the marginal-gain rule ties with N=1 when V is low (V=2) and beats the best fixed N by 0.2 to 5.9 units/task as V grows. The rule turns into an **attempt limit K*(V)** (1, 3, 5, 10, 23, 32 for V=2…100) that adapts to the task by stopping at the first acceptance.
- **P1 not supported — and the reason matters:** `fixed-1` has the lowest cost per accepted task (1.67) because it accepts only the easy ones (acceptance 66%). Cost per accepted task **cannot be the only metric**; it must come together with the acceptance rate or utility. Against fixed-3/5/9 the elastic rule is cheaper **and** accepts more.
- **P3 partially supported:** the easy class moves to 2–3 agents with V ≥ 15 (10% failure × high value pays for one retry) — wrong prediction; coupled becomes macro only with V ≥ 70.
- **P4 not supported by the criterion (partially in the predicted direction):** the controller that ignores correlation over-expands (cost/accepted 3.63 vs 2.73 at V=20) and raises false acceptance (4.6% vs 2.8%); at V=100 both hit the 32-attempt cap and tie.
- **P5 not supported by the criterion (partially):** the probe helps little (+0.06–0.07 utility at V=10–20) and stops helping at V ≥ 50 (the controller already tries everything). The value of estimating difficulty up front depends on V.
- **False acceptance grows with expansion** (fixed-1 0.7% → elastic V=100 4.6%): the same effect as in `sim-verifier` — every additional attempt is a new chance for the test to let an error through.

## Limitations
- Sequential attempts conditionally independent given (class, S); no learning between attempts (an LLM agent sees the previous failure), no decomposition of coupled tasks, no latency cost in the utility.
- The controller knows the generating model (except in `elastic-mis`); in practice, ΔP must be estimated from data.
- **What needs validation with real LLMs:** the curve P(correct on attempt k+1 | k failures) by task type; the real specificity of acceptance tests; whether K*(V) estimated on a calibration set generalises to held-out tasks.
