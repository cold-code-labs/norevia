## Hypothesis (preregistered before the run)
Registered in `results/sims/PREREGISTRATION.jsonl` (2026-11-07T22:33:11Z, v1.0.0, code sha256 `0458aeae79c2bf83…`).

An imperfect verifier with high specificity and errors uncorrelated with the generators reduces false success below the vote and the single agent; but, since every extra attempt is a new chance to accept an error, the false success of best-of-N grows with N, and a weak verifier, or one correlated with task difficulty, can be worse than the vote.

- **P1** — With spec ≥ 0.95 and ρ_v=0, bon-seq has lower false success (FS) than vote-N and than single-high (same generation budget) for every N ≥ 3 and every ρ (disjoint 95% CIs).
- **P2** — The FS of bon-seq is non-decreasing in N (tolerance 1 p.p.); with ρ=ρ_v=0 it matches FS(N) = r_w/(r_c+r_w)·(1−(1−r_c−r_w)^N), r_c=p·sens, r_w=(1−p)(1−spec), within the 95% CI in ≥ 90% of the points.
- **P3** — A verifier correlated with difficulty (ρ_v=0.5) increases the FS of bon-seq vs ρ_v=0 for ρ ≥ 0.3, N ≥ 3, spec < 0.99.
- **P4** — With spec=0.5 and ρ=0, bon-seq has higher FS than the vote at N=33.
- **P5** — With ρ_v=0, the accuracy advantage of bon-seq over the vote at N=9 grows with ρ (0 < 0.3 < 0.6) for spec ≥ 0.8.

## Model
- Generators as in `sim-correlated-vote` (p=0.6, latent factor S with correlation ρ). Binary task.
- Verifier: accepts a correct candidate with prob. `sens`; accepts a wrong candidate with prob. 1−`spec`. With ρ_v>0, acceptance of wrong candidates depends on the same S (hard tasks fool the generator **and** the verifier).
- Strategies: `single-low`, `single-high` (effort N, p=1−0.4/√N), `vote` (N), `bon-seq` (stops at the 1st accepted; abstains if none), `bon-fallback` (if none is accepted, delivers the vote).
- False success = wrong delivery declared a success. Abstention is an honest failure (not false success).
- Cost = generations + c_v·verifications, c_v ∈ {0.2; 1}. 10,000 tasks per cell; 48 cells × 7 values of N.

## Interpretation
- The verifier is a source of information **independent** of the generators; that is why it escapes the vote's correlation ceiling. With spec ≥ 0.95, bon-seq has lower FS and lower cost per correct answer than the vote in every cell with that spec (with spec=0.5 this reverses, see P4).
- The FS of bon-seq **grows with N** and saturates at r_w/(r_c+r_w): more attempts increase the chance of accepting an error. The closed-form formula matched the MC at 55/56 points (ρ=ρ_v=0).
- **P1 failed** in 3 of 120 comparisons: with ρ=0.6 and N ≥ 17, the single agent with effort N (p≈0.90–0.93 under the assumed curve) has lower FS than bon-seq with spec=0.95. That is, with highly correlated errors and return to effort β=0.5, investing the budget in a single agent can be safer than sampling a lot.
- **P5 not supported:** the advantage of bon-seq over the vote is not monotone in ρ — it grows from ρ=0 to 0.3 and falls at 0.6, because with high ρ the hard tasks have almost no correct candidate for the verifier to approve.
- **P3 partial:** a correlated verifier increases FS in 53/60 comparisons; the exceptions are all with spec=0.5 and ρ=0.6, where FS is already high in both cases.
- `bon-fallback` (delivering the vote when nothing passes) masks failures: with small N the FS returns to the vote's level. Abstaining and escalating is the option that preserves verifiable acceptance.

## Limitations
- Binary verifier with fixed sens/spec and fixed cost; in LLMs, sens/spec depend on the task and the verifier may share biases with the generator beyond what ρ_v models.
- The effort curve of `single-high` is an assumption (β=0.5).
- **What needs validation with real LLMs:** sens/spec of real verifiers (unit tests vs LLM-as-judge) by task type; correlation between judge error and generator error; observed FS of best-of-N as a function of N.
